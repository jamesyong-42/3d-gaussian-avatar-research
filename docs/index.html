01The objective

What we want to achieve

Create a recognizable, full-body digital person from ordinary photos—and make that person behave like an interactive avatar, not a prerecorded reconstruction.

CREATE

Low-friction identity

A user uploads photos of the same person and outfit. A reconstruction job produces a saved, reusable avatar with enough information for future poses.

PERFORM

One avatar, many signals

Joint controls, motion clips, head-and-hand targets, or camera tracking drive the same avatar. Recording and replay preserve the resolved performance.

SHARE

Local, free-view rendering

A receiver obtains the asset, then compact motion updates. Each viewer can choose a viewpoint and render the moving avatar locally.

Why prove this on the web first?

The browser makes each boundary inspectable: the saved representation, the rig, the controls, the deformation result, and the packets. We can compare native and browser geometry, replay the same motion, and open a second receiver before adding headset calibration, stereo rendering, and engine integration.

Our success criterionA generated avatar must survive outside its originating model process: reload it, apply a previously unseen pose, drive it with real Mixamo motion, record it, and reproduce the performance on another browser using the same asset and pose contract.

The MVP is not a claim of studio-quality identity, physically simulated clothing, semantic facial capture, production telepresence, or standalone Quest performance. Those need separate evidence and budgets.

02The research landscape

“State of the art” depends on the question.

Input effort, body coverage, appearance quality, and runtime requirements define different research problems. A beautiful head avatar, a relightable studio capture, and a portable full-body photo reconstruction are not interchangeable solutions.

Primary sources for each lane are linked in the paper ledger below. The wider survey remains in our research atlas; the executable shortlist is in the generation experiment plan.

What the frontier is improving

Stronger human priors, multiple real observations, structured UV representations, generative completion, pose-dependent corrections, expressive faces, and relighting. These target different failure modes.

What our decision must optimize

Recognizability and motion quality at an acceptable capture, asset, and device cost. We need released models, inspectable outputs, faithful deformation, and reproducible browser evidence.

No universal winner has been established herePaper benchmarks use different captures, metrics, resolutions, and hardware. Our bundled-image experiments establish engineering feasibility—not a held-out identity-quality ranking or a new state-of-the-art claim.

Foundations and our executable generation path

P01Inherited foundation

3D Gaussian Splatting

Kerbl et al. · SIGGRAPH 2023 · representation foundation

Idea
Represent a scene with explicit, anisotropic Gaussian primitives instead of querying a radiance network for every sample.
Tech
Optimized centers, covariance, opacity and appearance; adaptive density control; a visibility-aware splatting rasterizer.
Learn
Explicit primitives can be stored and rendered efficiently, but static reconstruction does not supply a human rig.

Our decisionAdopt the representation through the upstream avatar models and Spark. We did not invent Gaussian splatting or reproduce the original scene-training pipeline.

Paper & author project

P02Inherited rig

SMPL-X

Pavlakos et al. · CVPR 2019 · expressive body prior

Idea
Use one parameterized model for body shape, articulated hands and facial expression.
Tech
A learned body model with a skeletal hierarchy, blend shapes and skinning; SMPLify-X is the paper’s image-fitting method.
Learn
A common body vocabulary can connect reconstruction and motion, but coordinate conventions and expression dimensions still differ between implementations.

Our decisionInherit the model-specific rig and deformation data from LHM / LHM++. Define our portable schema around them. The skeleton, anatomical prior and skinning mathematics are not ours.

Paper, model & terms

P03Main-app baseline

LHM

Large Animatable Human Reconstruction Model · 2025

Idea
Infer an animatable, full-body Gaussian avatar from a single image in a feed-forward pass.
Tech
Image-feature conditioning and a human-structured transformer predict canonical Gaussian attributes; the body prior provides animation structure.
Learn
The model already supplies the reconstruction and rigging ingredients. The engineering opportunity is to extract and preserve them beyond its native renderer.

Our decisionUse the official LHM-500M-HF checkpoint by default; expose 1B-HF and shape estimation as controlled alternatives. Our exporter and browser implementation are custom; the pretrained generator is upstream.

PaperOfficial codeOur exporter

P04Main-app option

LHM++

Pose-free image reconstruction · 2025 / revised March 2026

Idea
Use one or multiple pose-free photographs to recover a more detailed, animatable human.
Tech
Point/image feature fusion, shape prediction and a Gaussian representation; the broader paper also includes a neural animation renderer.
Learn
Additional real views can constrain hidden regions. A native neural-enhanced result is not automatically the same product as a portable Gaussian-only render.

Our decisionIntegrate LHMPP-700M-PixelShuffle, 1–8 photos and 160K splats. Export its native shape and bindings, compact the runtime asset and animate with WebGPU. We do not port the paper’s complete neural renderer.

P05Isolated browser lab

IDOL

Instant Photorealistic 3D Human Creation · CVPR 2025

Idea
Predict a structured human representation from one photo, using an SMPL-X UV layout to organize Gaussian attributes.
Tech
A high-resolution image encoder, UV-Alignment Transformer and UV decoder produce an animatable canonical representation.
Learn
A different reconstruction prior can require a genuinely different runtime. Shared joint names do not imply identical deformation.

Our decisionImplement a separate exporter, compact correctives, exact posed-neighbor sizes, full covariance and a GPU-resident diagnostic renderer. Native/browser geometry passes; main-app uploads, Mixamo and remote adapters remain unintegrated.

Paper & projectOfficial codeOur lab

P06Conditional challenger

DiGS-Avatar

UV-space diffusion completion · ECCV 2026

Idea
Complete a 3D-consistent UV latent from a single image, instead of first synthesizing independent views.
Tech
A multi-view teacher supervises a single-view diffusion student; semantic feature refinement precedes Gaussian decoding.
Learn
A stronger completion prior may improve unseen regions, but plausible invented detail must be measured separately from recovered identity.

Our decisionKeep in the independent-prior experiment lane. Our September 8 readiness audit did not establish a usable pretrained dependency set; no local generation or browser trial is claimed. Recheck release readiness before execution.

P07Architectural influence

Mon3tr

Monocular 3D telepresence · January 2026

Idea
Amortize expensive avatar creation offline, then send a compact live performance to a receiver that animates and renders locally.
Tech
Multi-view personalized fitting; a personalized parametric mesh and learned mesh/Gaussian corrections; monocular live tracking and a compact WebRTC stream.
Learn
Separate identity from motion, and separate avatar-building input from live tracking input. “Monocular” describes live capture—not one-photo avatar creation.

Our decisionAdopt the asset-once / motion-continuously principle. We do not reproduce its personalized fitting, corrective networks, tracking stack or mobile transport implementation. Deferral reflects scope and readiness, not a benchmark defeat.

P08Control / VR reference

VRGaussianAvatar

Integrating 3D Gaussian Avatars into VR · February 2026

Idea
Connect an image-generated Gaussian avatar to headset-driven embodiment and evaluate the integrated VR experience.
Tech
Off-the-shelf LHM-500M reconstruction; Final IK from head/hand signals; a Python backend returns stereo JPEGs to Unity. Binocular Batching shares work across both eye views.

04Our design

Borrow the reconstruction.
Design the portable system around it.

The durable boundary is an avatar asset plus a resolved pose. Everything before that boundary creates identity or interprets signals; everything after it reproduces the chosen model’s animation and renders the result.

INHERITED

Research and libraries

Pretrained LHM / LHM++ generation; the SMPL-X body prior and rig; Gaussian representation and skinning mathematics; Spark / Three.js rendering; MediaPipe perception; Mixamo motion data.

DESIGNED & IMPLEMENTED HERE

Portable integration

Model-aware exports and asset contracts; compact packaging; native-matching CPU/WebGPU deformation; signal and Mixamo adapters; resolved-pose replay; GSW2 and buffering; application, provenance and validation tooling.

STILL TO ESTABLISH

Quality and device proof

A consented cross-model benchmark; calibrated facial semantics; robust body/contact control; IDOL app integration; production transport; Unity/Meta parity, stereo and sustained target-device budgets.

Actual model configuration: generation_config.py · experiment record · IDOL boundary.

We use upstream code and pretrained weights with recorded revisions and hashes. We have not trained or fine-tuned the generation networks. For LHM, predicted Gaussian offsets still reconstruct geometry and appearance even with zero body-shape betas; “zero shape” does not mean the avatar is only an unmodified template. Estimated shape changes both exported geometry and rest-joint proportions, and estimator failure is reported rather than silently replaced with zero.

  1. Normalize and bound the upload.Accept JPG, PNG or WebP; decode pixels, apply EXIF orientation, convert RGB, and fit within 2048 × 2048. Enforce at least 128 pixels per side, at most 24 MP, 16 MB per file and 64 MB combined. LHM accepts one image; LHM++ accepts up to eight. Client filenames do not become filesystem paths.
  2. Execute the appropriate native adapter.LHM uses the existing workstation CUDA environment. LHM++ uses an isolated Linux CUDA container with networking disabled, read-only model/input mounts, a shared GPU lock and bounded execution. Model configuration and errors remain visible.
  3. Capture the real deformation inputs.The LHM exporter intercepts the native inference call without editing vendor source. It extracts shape-adjusted Gaussian data, the actual hierarchy, bindings and expression directions—not a separately guessed auto-rig.
  4. Generate native references and publish provenance.Export seven full center/orientation pose fixtures, byte layouts and checksums. Record model, checkpoint, source/exporter and normalized-input hashes, shape parameters and settings. Publish only when the required validation passes.
Operational bounds for the LHM++ generation route

The implementation uses a 360-second container timeout, 120-second GPU-lock wait and 15 GiB configured-disk reserve. It records exact image IDs and job labels, retains failed evidence, and only stops a container whose recorded job identity matches. Readiness checks are read-only; they do not install or start Docker. The main app’s queue is bounded, and concurrent API uploads cannot bypass its post-read capacity check.

5.2 / The artifact is more than a PLY

A conventional static PLY provides splat appearance in a particular configuration. Our browser also needs the rig, bindings and deformation semantics. We package these as a JSON manifest plus aligned, little-endian typed arrays; the loader verifies the complete binary’s byte length and SHA-256.

Asset componentWhat it preservesWhy it matters
Gaussian attributesZero-pose centers, predicted quaternions, activated scales, RGB and opacity.These are runtime values, not untreated PLY logits or log-scales.
Rig and shape55 named joints, parents, rest positions and the chosen shape parameters.Motion must be interpreted against the correct skeleton and body proportions.
Exact sparse skinningCSR offsets, bone indices and every nonzero weight.No top-four pruning or new normalization changes the native model.

LHM++ requires its own export logic even though it can use the same validated browser math. Its diffused volumetric skinning weights are evaluated at the predicted offsets, with native fixed bindings retained for hands and face. Baking is permitted only after checking that expression directions outside those fixed regions are zero. Unlike original LHM, this adapter exports no constrained-body rotation locks.

5.3 / Compact the representation without changing its math

Version 2 omits expression rows that are numerically zero for every coefficient, retains all nonzero float32 values, and moves native reference arrays out of the normal playback download. This is exact numeric sparsity and packaging—not learned compression, skin-weight pruning or quantization.

5.4 / Source photos remain attached to the saved result

Opening a saved avatar restores its ordered normalized input photos and supported generation settings. The API exposes a bounded source-photo catalog and numeric image routes; legacy research inputs require explicit mapping and recorded hashes. The UI distinguishes saved inputs from a new selection, clears stale previews on avatar changes, and prevents late responses or missing images from silently producing the wrong regeneration input set.

This is provenance and product workflow, not part of LHM’s reconstruction method. Photos and avatar data persist locally; replacing the creation-panel selection does not rewrite a saved avatar. See source_photos.py and the September 10 verification.

5.5 / How we choose the next generator

Executed engineering experiments

LHM 500M/1B × zero/estimated shape across three bundled inputs; LHM++ 1/4/8-view export and browser trials; IDOL native reconstruction, compact correction and full browser geometry in an isolated lab.

The research decision still missing

A consented, held-out identity and motion benchmark. Keep photo count, preprocessing, shape fitting and renderer controlled; report native quality and portable quality separately.

Start with three smoke subjects, then a reserved ten-subject decision set. For the multi-photo lane, use nested 1/4/8-image inputs and independent holdout views. Evaluate silhouette, face/hair/clothing detail, unseen regions, motion artifacts, creation cost, asset size and sustained client performance. Use pixel metrics only with properly aligned references; compare different models through images or geometry, not pointwise Gaussian indices.

Portability is a gate, not a file extensionIDOL demonstrates why: pose correctives, native hand conventions, fresh posed-neighbor scale estimation and full covariance required a new runtime. Its 99 passing geometry cases do not automatically make it compatible with our Mixamo, recording, remote or upload paths.

Sparse-target IK is our adapter—not LHM

The bounded solver performs up to 12 iterations per chain with coarse joint clamps. Its inputs represent calibrated joint anchors, not raw headset/controller origins. A future device adapter must supply the user calibration and device-to-joint offsets.

Tracking is not semantic facial control

MediaPipe supplies perception; our code converts it to avatar signals. A detected facial coefficient cannot simply be copied into LHM’s unrelated learned expression basis. A validated semantic mapping is a separate design task.

Camera scheduling, privacy and limits

The camera is explicitly enabled by the user. Tracking runs locally in a separate worker with 640 × 480 input, at most one frame in flight, an approximately 85 ms submission throttle and a 500 ms stale threshold. Switching away releases the device. The prototype does not transmit webcam frames as its avatar transport, does not capture audio, and does not establish reliable body position from monocular video.

6.3 / Reuse skeletal animation through retargeting

Yes—Mixamo motion can animate the Gaussian avatar because the model has an articulated body rig. We use the FBX skeleton and motion only: the source mesh and textures are not rendered, and the avatar does not need to be uploaded to Mixamo or generated again.

We preserve the target’s rest-joint positions and bone lengths. Source animated rotations are measured relative to the actual bind pose, transformed into a common anatomical frame, and corrected for the source/target rest stance. The first dancing frame is never assumed to be a T-pose.

G(t) = C · Q(t) · B⁻¹ · C⁻¹ · AQ(t): source world rotation · B: source bind rotation · C: coordinate alignment · A: rest-stance correction. Convert desired world rotations G(t) into target-local rotations through the target hierarchy.

Hips displacement is scaled by the target/source mean leg length. X/Z travel is centered on the first frame; vertical motion is relative to the source bind pose. “In place” removes horizontal travel but preserves vertical motion. Jaw, eyes and the 100 expression coefficients remain independent of the 52 mapped body/finger joints.

Import boundaries and unsupported motion

Parsing and the 30 Hz bake occur in a local worker; textures are ignored and FBX files are not uploaded. Limits are 64 MiB per file, 12 files per batch, 16 takes / five minutes per file and a 45-second conversion timeout. A matching source-character T-pose can repair unavailable rest information for future imports. Mirrored/non-uniform skeleton scale, animated scale and translations below the hips are rejected.

The library is in memory. There is no crossfade, animation state machine, foot planting, collision response, root-motion accumulation across loops or secondary clothing physics. Retargeting uses standard geometric ideas; our contribution is the concrete rest-aware adapter and its verification.

6.4 / Reproduce the native deformation—not a plausible substitute

For each Gaussian, the worker blends the exported joint transforms using every stored skinning influence. It then applies expression displacement, the model’s orientation rule and the separate actor-root transform.

P = Σⱼ wⱼ Tⱼ
center = actorRoot · P · [x₀ + E e, 1]
rotation = rootQ · normalize(matrixToQuaternion(P.linear · N)) · q₀P: weighted posed joint transform · x₀: exported zero-pose center · E/e: expression directions/coefficients · N: exported neutral linear transform · q₀: predicted Gaussian orientation.

Model-specific details are part of the contract

For LHM’s constrained body points, omit the middle orientation term. The matrix-to-quaternion conversion follows PyTorch3D’s largest-candidate algorithm even when the blended matrix is non-orthogonal. Scales, colors and opacity stay as exported.

Correct implementation ≠ physical truth

We match LHM’s chosen orientation and deformation model. We do not replace it with a different covariance-transport rule, nor claim that exact matching creates realistic cloth, anatomical limits or accurate unseen appearances.

Seven native fixtures compare all centers and orientations before renderer packing. The acceptance thresholds are less than 1 mm center error and less than 0.002 radians orientation error. Additional CPU/GPU stress poses exercise all expressions and actor-root transforms. These test portability, not cross-model identity quality or pixel-identical rasterization.

6.5 / A browser runtime with explicit performance boundaries

The worker attempts WebGPU automatically for assets with at least 100K splats. The 40K LHM baseline remains on CPU by default. Initialization, limit, device-loss or verification failures report a reason and fall back to the CPU reference without substituting a different skinning approximation. Only one deformation request is in flight; render refresh and pose-update scheduling are measured separately.

Spark and Three.js provide the splat and scene rendering infrastructure. Our integration updates the Gaussian centers and rotations through texture-aligned extended splat data. The viewport can refresh between new deformations: approximately 55 display FPS with 30 pose updates per second is not 55 newly deformed poses per second.

6.6 / Record the result, not the controller instructions

Recording stores resolved poses, including the actor root, joint rotations, expressions and timing. This makes replay independent of the original procedural clock, webcam inference, IK solution or FBX importer. A download includes asset identity and checksum so the performance can be associated with its avatar.

Continuous motionInterpolate root and articulation from the same pair of snapshots. Convert rotations to quaternions and use shortest-arc slerp.
Discontinuous motionOn seeks, clip changes, loop wraps or teleports, hold / jump across the boundary instead of fabricating a slide through unrelated poses.

Current recordings are capped at 60 seconds and remain in memory until downloaded as JSON. The MVP has no recording-import UI or persistent motion library. Main-app record/replay is not yet an IDOL feature.

New design work beyond the MVPSemantic face calibration; robust full-body solving with confidence and contact constraints; motion blending and state transitions; head/body seam handling if a specialist is added; and target-device deformation / rendering budgets. None is automatically solved by generating a rigged avatar.

07Pillar III / network transport

Distribute the identity.
Stream only the performance.

The network carries two kinds of data with different lifetimes: a large, stable avatar asset and small, frequent pose snapshots. No live pixels or per-Gaussian positions are sent through the pose relay.

7.1 / GSW2: the wire representation we designed

The main app encodes the resolved pose as a versioned, little-endian binary snapshot. The asset’s expression semantics remain model-specific; packet versioning does not turn them into a universal facial vocabulary.

7.2 / Receiver behavior is part of the animation design

  1. Resolve identity first.A reliable room message announces the avatar identity. Download and checksum-validate the asset before accepting motion for it.
  2. Keep snapshots coherent.Reject stale sequences and invalid packets. Never combine the root from one instant with limbs from another.
  3. Buffer by arrival time.The current receiver uses an 80 ms buffer and one snapshot pair for the entire body. The carried sender timestamp is diagnostic; no cross-machine clock synchronization is claimed.
  4. Handle gaps and discontinuities explicitly.Hold the newest complete pose when data runs out. A teleport clears interpolation history. Do not invent motion across a clip seek or unrelated timeline segment.
What this transport experiment does not proveWebSocket is reliable and ordered. The latency / jitter / loss controls are application-level impairment experiments, not a reproduction of unreliable WebRTC or UDP behavior. There is no extrapolation, clock-drift correction, reconnect/resend protocol or production congestion tuning.

7.3 / What carries forward—and what needs a new design

Carry forward the separation of identity and motion, explicit coordinate/schema semantics, complete snapshots, native-deformation parity and discontinuity handling. For an Internet product, establish multi-user authorization, asset-cache policy, reconnect behavior, pose age and clock handling, bounded send queues, congestion behavior and a transport choice appropriate to loss and latency. WebRTC data channels are a candidate, not an implemented feature.

The older Python/C# experiment used a different 50-expression packet. It is not wire-compatible with current GSW2 and cannot count as proof of the current Unity receiver. Port the current contract and test vectors explicitly.

Password protection and Tailscale Funnel are access / routing layers described in operations. They do not change the avatar payload into a new transport protocol or establish WAN performance.

How to interpret the performance numbers

Keep model inference, whole-process generation, GPU memory, deformation work, delivered fresh poses and displayed frames separate. LHM job times include process overhead; sampled whole-GPU memory is not framework allocator memory. The IDOL 55.2 FPS resident path differs from its 9.4 FPS CPU-transfer/WebGL bridge and from its geometry-only timings. None of these measurements is a smartphone or Quest benchmark.

Native parity compares an asset against its own native deformation. A cross-model comparison must instead use appropriate image/geometry criteria. A limited smoke test on bundled inputs cannot establish general identity preservation.

09Roadmap and decision gates

What we must establish next

The browser is the executable reference, not the final proof of a headset product. Preserve what is already validated, and make each new claim pass its own gate.

GATE 01 / RESEARCH

Choose on held-out quality

Consented subjects, fixed input budgets, failure taxonomy and independent views. Decide whether LHM++ or IDOL earns a quality tier or replaces the baseline.

GATE 02 / PORTABILITY

Complete model / engine adapters

IDOL motion and app integration; then C# asset loading, FK and deformation against the current native fixtures and GSW2 tests.

GATE 03 / DEVICES

Calibrate and render in stereo

Device-to-joint offsets, handedness, tracking confidence, loss behavior, eye cameras, depth/compositing and sustained memory/frame budgets.

GATE 04 / SHARING

Prove an Internet session

Independent public reachability, authorization, reconnects, controlled WAN tests and pose-age / latency reporting. Add multi-user isolation before product use.

Access protection is not multi-user isolation

The separate gateway authenticates HTML, assets, uploads, API routes and WebSocket upgrades. It uses host/origin checks and Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite session cookies. It is a shared-password research preview: trusted testers with access can see the saved library and submit jobs. It is not an account system, tenant boundary or proof of production hardening.

GitHub Pages hosts this static architecture document only. It does not run Python, accept photo uploads, host the avatar library, or expose a GPU. A live preview is a separate, explicitly authenticated deployment; no existing private endpoint is published here.

Personal data and third-party rightsUploaded and normalized photos, generated avatars and provenance persist locally; there is no automatic retention/deletion policy. Camera processing and FBX parsing remain local, but a shared preview exposes saved data to authorized testers. Use consented inputs. Review code, checkpoint, SMPL-X/FLAME, training-data and motion-asset terms separately before distribution or commercial use; an upstream repository license alone does not settle the whole dependency chain.

Glossary

Gaussian / splat
An oriented, soft 3D primitive with position, scale, appearance and opacity, projected as an ellipse for rendering.
Canonical / rest space
The reference configuration in which identity attributes and animation bindings are defined. An exporter must state the exact convention.
Rig
A skeleton plus the rules and data connecting pose parameters to visible geometry. A rig does not infer motion by itself.
Skinning / LBS
Linear blend skinning combines joint transforms according to per-point weights.
FK / IK
Forward kinematics computes joint locations from rotations; inverse kinematics estimates rotations that reach targets.
Retargeting
Transferring motion between rigs while accounting for rest poses, axes, proportions and supported channels.
Correctives
Additional pose- or expression-dependent changes beyond basic skinning. Their model-specific meaning must be preserved.
UV representation
A 2D coordinate layout tied to a body surface, used by some generators to organize features or Gaussian attributes.
Resolved pose
The complete animation state after input interpretation, shared by rendering, recording and transport.
Pose Hz / display FPS
New deformation updates per second versus displayed frames per second. They can differ substantially.
Native parity
Agreement with the originating model’s numerical output within a frozen tolerance—not proof of visual quality or physical correctness.
